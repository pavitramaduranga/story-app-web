7 Story Time Questions to Get Your Preschooler Talking

Use small pauses before, during and after a story to invite ideas, feelings and playful conversation—without turning reading into a quiz.

Written by the Baboo Stories team · About 7 min read

Story time is a chance to share a book or story and notice what your child is thinking. For children ages 2–5, story time questions for preschoolers work best as invitations: a child might answer with a sentence, point to a picture, repeat one word or simply listen.

You do not need to ask all seven questions. One warm pause can be enough. If your child wants you to keep reading, keep reading.

Before reading: let your child have a say

1. “Which story shall we read?”

When: Before opening the story. Offer two or three choices. If your child points, say, “You chose the one with the moon.” If they answer with one word, reflect it: “The farm story. Good choice.” If they just listen, choose calmly and begin.

2. “What do you notice on the cover?”

When: Before the first page. Notice one detail yourself if your child is quiet: “I see a small yellow boat. I wonder where it might go.” Pointing or looking closely is a complete response.

During reading: pause for noticing and imagining

3. “What do you think happens next?”

When: At a natural pause before a character makes a choice. Accept any answer: “You think the rabbit will hide. Let’s turn the page.” If your child wants to listen, offer your own guess and continue.

4. “How do you think she feels?”

When: When a character's face, body or situation changes. Your child may say “sad,” make a face or point. You can respond, “Maybe unsure. She might want someone to say hello.” Do not treat one emotion as the only correct answer.

5. “What could he say to ask to join in?”

When: When a character wants to enter a game. Model a short line: “Could I play too?” If your child says one word, expand it gently: “Play—yes, he could say, ‘Can I play?’”

After reading: connect the story to your child

6. “What was your favourite part?”

When: At the end, or later while drawing or playing. A favourite part might be a character, sound or tiny detail. If your child points, name what you see: “The red boots were your favourite.”

7. “How would you end the story?”

When: After the original ending, or when your child wants to change it. “The boat goes home,” “the dragon eats toast” and “it ends here” are all fine. A child who wants to listen can hear your version.

Keep the conversation light

  • Ask one question, then wait.
  • Accept pointing, gestures, sounds, one-word answers and silence.
  • Respond to the idea your child offers instead of correcting it.
  • Stop asking when your child wants to return to the story.

Questions parents often ask

How many questions should I ask during story time?

One pause can be enough. Let your child answer with words, pointing, a sound or attention.

What if my preschooler does not answer?

Answer gently yourself, offer a choice or keep reading. Listening and watching are valid ways to join story time.
